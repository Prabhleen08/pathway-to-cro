About this project

Project note

The lesson I want to carry forward is to say what I want out loud and be clear about what I bring. At a Tech Week event, I asked the CRO of a streaming company what it would take for me to become his chief of staff. We met afterward, and I realized I didn't want to work at his company. Still, the connection could become a mentor or thought partner as I work toward becoming a CRO. At the same event, I connected an account-based marketing founder with my company's vendor team; his firm later won a contract, and I asked him to mentor me about board service. Those encounters showed me that direct asks can create value in both directions.

My Leverage Inventory showed Networks (+1.00) and Allocentrism (+0.95) as strengths, with Exchange (-1.05) as my stretch. The Triple Helix exercise showed me what that looks like: I took $26M with the best seat, then stopped because I didn't want to squeeze two people I liked. My pattern: I ask easily when it's for my own growth, and I soften when the ask costs someone I like. At work, I also learned to pair pipeline numbers with how a goal might affect the sales team.

This month I tested the opposite. I told my department heads I wanted to be closer to the P&L, and I now have a seat in planning across finance, sales, and marketing. I also cold-emailed D2, a women-in-AI community, and I'm attending my first meeting on Nov 10 with a speaking invite.

To keep this going, I draw a card before any meeting where I need something. On the first Sunday of each month, I write one journal page: my win, what I asked for and offered, and where I softened. Monthly is the pace I'll actually keep during WEMBA. In May 2027, I'll reread all of it before graduation.
